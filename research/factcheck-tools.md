# Fact-check: interactive tools and guides

Scope: `components/tools/WaterTestDecoder.tsx`, `TankCalculator.tsx`, `SchedulePlanner.tsx`, `HurricaneChecklist.tsx`, `lib/guides.ts`, `lib/faqs.ts`, and the FAQ constants in `app/tools/water-test`, `tank-volume`, `service-planner`. Checked 2026-09-27. No files edited.

Verdict key: **Correct** / **Slightly off** (defensible but imprecise) / **Wrong** / **Unverifiable** (no authoritative source found; professional judgment).

Sources used most: Randy Holmes-Farley (RHF) "Reef Aquarium Water Parameters" (reefkeeping.com 2004-05) and his 2023 Reef2Reef nutrient-target article; Instant Ocean and Reef Crystals label text (instantocean.com); reefcalcs.com salt table (cites manufacturer datasheets); City of Boca Raton 2024 CCR and Water Quality page; PBCWUD 2024/2025 CCR and water-quality page; Aquarium Co-Op power-outage article; Texas A&M extension nitrite sheet; aquariumscience.org ammonia pages. Note: reefkeeping.com refused connections during this check (ECONNREFUSED); RHF articles hosted there are cited from search-index excerpts and his Reef2Reef republications.

## Summary of what actually needs changing

1. **Salt mix math is off from reef salinity.** 35 g/L = 0.29 lb/gal = "half a cup" matches the Instant Ocean *label* rate, and that label rate mixes to **sg 1.020-1.024 (about 1.022)**, not the 1.026 / 35 ppt the tool claims. To hit 1.026 you need roughly 0.34-0.36 lb (about 40-42 g/L, about 0.6 cup) per gallon with Instant Ocean; Red Sea Coral Pro is about 41 g/L; Reef Crystals about 0.33 lb/gal.
2. **Local hardness claims are wrong.** Boca Raton's finished water is **65-80 mg/L total hardness (3.8-4.7 grains, about 3.6-4.5 dGH), pH 8.0-8.5**, after membrane plus lime softening. The decoder says GH above 20 dGH is "normal for Palm Beach County tap water" and KH above 12 is "typical of local tap water"; both are wrong for Boca and unsupported for PBCWUD. The tap-water guide's "hard, with high calcium and alkalinity" should become "moderately hard".
3. **Saltwater nitrite is not "same urgency as ammonia."** RHF: nitrite is far less toxic in marine systems than freshwater; it is a cycle indicator, not an emergency.
4. **Saltwater ammonia "fix now" threshold should be 0.25 ppm, not 0.5.** At reef pH (8.1-8.3) about 8-10% of total ammonia is free NH3; 0.5 ppm total puts NH3 at about 0.05 ppm, the gill-damage level. RHF's reef recommendation is under 0.1 ppm.
5. **Hurricane "during the outage" step contradicts mainstream guidance**: Aquarium Co-Op says do not change water during an outage; the checklist tells people to swap water every few hours. Also "cloudy haze means oxygen is running low" is the wrong mechanism (cloudiness is a bacterial bloom or die-off; gasping is the oxygen sign).
6. **Aquifer wording.** Boca Raton draws from the Biscayne Aquifer (city CCR). Boynton Beach describes its source as the "East Coast Surficial Aquifer"; PBCWUD's eastern area is Biscayne. Say "the shallow surficial aquifer system (the Biscayne Aquifer in Boca Raton)".
7. Minor: salinity target should be 1.025-1.026 (34-35 ppt) with 1.026 as the number; phosphate floor 0.03 not 0.02; magnesium 1250-1350 (1400 is harmless but not the reference); reef pH "bad" floor 7.8 not 7.7; freshwater nitrate "bad" at 60 is more conservative than Aquarium Co-Op (80-100) and planted tanks deliberately run 20-50 ppm.

Everything else checks out or is professional judgment that no source contradicts.
